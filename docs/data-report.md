# Data report: companion zones from invented claims

Generated by `python -m analysis` from `build/claims.csv` (written by `python -m datagen`). Every car, shop, vehicle, claim and part number here is invented.

## What this re-tells

A parts dealer turned five years of accident-repair sales into quote logic: when a caller says "front bumper", which other zones usually come with it? This repo re-tells that with invented data. The generator (`datagen/generate.py`) encodes only mechanisms - impact face, point, width and depth; zones at invented distances and layers; damage fading with distance and layer; per-part min severity from the catalog; repair instead of replacement; hidden damage found after disassembly; consumables. It contains no co-occurrence rates. The shares below fall out of those mechanisms, which is the point of the exercise *and* its weakest spot (see Caveats).

## The table (`app/data/zones.json`)

- 108 cells: 81 model cells and 27 all-models (`*`) cells.
- 36 model cells have fewer than 20 cases and use the all-models cell (`scope: all`, `n` = all-models n): Atto 6 of 27, Vera 9 of 27, Ridge 21 of 27.
- 3 all-models cells are themselves below 20 cases and are shipped as they are: front/both/minor (n=7), rear/both/minor (n=4), side/both/severe (n=19).
- 0 cells have no zone at or above 0.3.
- A cell exists only where data exists. Some combinations never occur (e.g. front/none/moderate: a moderate front hit always breaks some sided part), so the app needs its own fallback for those.

## Backtest

Time split: train on cases starting before 2025-01-01 (2386 cases), test on the rest (529 cases). For each test case the anchor zone (most lines, ties in catalog order) stands in for what the caller said; the trained table predicts the companion zones (anchor excluded), compared with the case's other zones. Micro-averaged over all test cases.

| variant | threshold | judged on | cases | predicted | correct | actual | precision | recall | fallback cells |
|---|---:|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| merge 30 d + drop consumables (shipped) | 0.30 | merged cases | 448 | 1430 | 913 | 1023 | 0.64 | 0.89 | 123 |
| merge 30 d + drop consumables (shipped) | 0.50 | merged cases | 448 | 1002 | 751 | 1023 | 0.75 | 0.73 | 123 |
| no merge (every claim its own case) | 0.30 | merged cases | 448 | 1361 | 881 | 1023 | 0.65 | 0.86 | 96 |
| no merge (every claim its own case) | 0.50 | merged cases | 448 | 1001 | 740 | 1023 | 0.74 | 0.72 | 96 |
| no merge, judged on its own claims | 0.30 | single claims | 548 | 1491 | 898 | 1046 | 0.60 | 0.86 | 108 |
| no merge, judged on its own claims | 0.50 | single claims | 548 | 1053 | 741 | 1046 | 0.70 | 0.71 | 108 |
| merge, consumables counted as lines | 0.30 | merged cases | 448 | 1525 | 922 | 1023 | 0.60 | 0.90 | 106 |
| merge, consumables counted as lines | 0.50 | merged cases | 448 | 936 | 710 | 1023 | 0.76 | 0.69 | 106 |
| baseline: every zone of the case's position | - | merged cases | 448 | 1786 | 776 | 1023 | 0.43 | 0.76 | 0 |

Reading it (precision / recall):

- **Threshold.** 0.30 gives 0.64 / 0.89, 0.50 gives 0.75 / 0.73: the lower threshold adds more companions, catching more of what the shop will need at the cost of lines that get deleted again. The app ships 0.30 because removing a line is easier than adding one.
- **Merge.** Training on single claims instead of merged cases gives 0.65 / 0.86 against the same merged test cases (0.64 / 0.89 with the merge). Judged on its own single claims it gives 0.60 / 0.86. The difference is small here because a supplement mostly adds hidden parts of zones already on the first claim: only 69 of 454 merged cases gain a zone from a later claim.
- **Consumables.** Counting them as lines gives 0.60 / 0.90. They never map to a zone, so they only move the severity thirds (and with them which cell a case lands in).

## Caveats

- **Circular by construction.** We wrote the generator, so the analysis can only find what its mechanisms imply. The guard is that the generator holds no target shares, only geometry and rules; but the numbers say nothing about real cars. What the exercise shows is that the pipeline (merge, consumables, cells, fallback, backtest) works and where it breaks.
- **Severity is optimistic.** Here severity is the tertile of the case's own line count, which already encodes how many zones were hit - the backtest peeks at the answer through it. In production severity comes from the caller's words ("scratch", "smashed"), which is noisier, so expect lower numbers.
- **The anchor is a stand-in.** The caller names the zone they see; we use the zone with the most lines, which may differ from what a person would say first.
- **Side `both` mostly means a centre hit** (brackets or lamps on both sides), not a caller saying "both sides". A caller who names no side maps to `none`, which only exists for light hits.
- **The 30-day merge cannot tell a supplement from a second accident** within the window. With the generator's fleet that happens rarely (see the merge check), in real data it depends on the fleet.
- The thirds are computed per position over all models, so a stiffer model (fewer broken parts per impact) lands in lower severities more often.
